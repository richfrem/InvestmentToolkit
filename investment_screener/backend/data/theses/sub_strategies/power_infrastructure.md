## 1. Executive Summary

AI training and inference demand is creating an unprecedented surge in electricity consumption. Data centers are expected to double their share of US power consumption by 2030. The bottleneck is not compute — it is dispatchable, carbon-controlled power at scale. This thesis owns the energy infrastructure stack: nuclear baseload (CEG), merchant power with nuclear + natgas (VST), distributed fuel cell generation (BE), micro-nuclear commercialization (OKLO), and AI-native power grid infrastructure (PSIX).

## 2. The Core Thesis (Why this? Why now?)

**The AI electricity problem**: GPT-4-class training runs consume ~50 GWh. Next-generation frontier models are estimated at 1–5 TWh. Data center electricity demand is growing faster than grid buildout. Hyperscalers (Microsoft, Google, Amazon) are signing 20-year nuclear PPAs because renewable intermittency cannot support always-on AI compute.

**Nuclear renaissance**: The first nuclear renaissance (2000s) failed on economics. This one is driven by hyperscaler demand guarantees. CEG's Three Mile Island restart (for Microsoft PPA) is the proof point. VST's nuclear-plus-natgas merchant fleet is uniquely positioned to capture peak-hour AI demand pricing.

**Distributed generation**: Bloom Energy (BE) fuel cells allow data centers to bypass the grid entirely — generating power on-site from natural gas or hydrogen. As grid interconnection queues stretch 5-10 years, on-site distributed generation becomes a premium service.

**Micro-nuclear**: OKLO's Aurora compact fast reactor targets 1-50 MW deployments — exactly the scale needed for data center campuses and defense installations. First commercial deployment targeted 2027-2028.

**Grid intelligence**: PSIX provides AI-native power infrastructure optimization — a necessary layer as grid complexity grows with intermittent renewables and distributed AI load.

- **Core Holdings**: BE (fuel cells), CEG (nuclear baseload), VST (merchant nuclear + natgas)
- **Speculative Growth**: OKLO (micro-nuclear), PSIX (AI power grid)
- **Exited**: EQT (natural gas E&P — repositioned into nuclear-direct names)

## 3. Adversarial Red-Team Review

- **Risk 1**: Nuclear regulatory delay. NRC timelines are unpredictable. OKLO's first deployment could slip 2-4 years. *Mitigation*: OKLO is sized as speculative (sub-2% target); CEG and VST provide near-term operating cashflow.
- **Risk 2**: Power prices normalize. Current merchant power prices (VST, CEG) are elevated. A mild winter + renewable overbuild could compress margins. *Mitigation*: Long-term PPAs (CEG Microsoft deal) lock in floor prices. VST's natgas optionality provides upside in high-demand periods.
- **Risk 3**: BE cash burn. Bloom Energy has historically operated near breakeven. A capital markets freeze could stress the balance sheet. *Mitigation*: Microsoft and Samsung as anchor customers; growing backlog provides visibility.
- **Risk 4**: PSIX liquidity. Small-cap with limited trading volume — position sizing must respect this. *Mitigation*: Capped at 2.3% target; no aggressive adds without volume confirmation.
- **Risk 5**: Policy reversal. A future administration could reverse nuclear subsidies or IRA energy credits. *Mitigation*: Hyperscaler PPA demand is the demand driver, not policy — private contracts survive administration changes.
