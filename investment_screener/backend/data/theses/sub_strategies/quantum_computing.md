## 2. The Core Thesis (Why this? Why now?)

**The quantum advantage window**: Classical computing approaches fundamental limits for certain problem classes (optimization, simulation, cryptography). Quantum systems exploit superposition and entanglement to solve these exponentially faster. The commercial "quantum advantage" milestone — where a quantum system solves a useful real-world problem faster than the best classical system — is now within 3-5 years for targeted applications.

**Government as first customer**: The US DoD, NSA, and DARPA are funding quantum development as a national security imperative. Post-quantum cryptography standardization (NIST PQC 2024) creates an immediate commercial pull for quantum-safe solutions. IONQ and Rigetti both have active government contracts.

**AI synergy**: Quantum-classical hybrid algorithms for AI training (quantum sampling for generative models, quantum optimization for neural architecture search) are an emerging research area. If quantum hardware achieves error rates below a critical threshold, it could accelerate AI training workloads dramatically.

**Technology differentiation**:
- **Trapped-ion** (IONQ): Highest gate fidelity, software-defined, cloud-accessible via AWS/Azure/Google. Most likely path to early commercial utility.
- **Quantum annealing** (D-Wave/QBTS): Solves optimization problems today. Real paying customers (Volkswagen, Lockheed, NatGrid). Different architecture — not universal but commercially deployed.
- **Superconducting** (RGTI, IBM): Faster gate speeds but lower fidelity. IBM's roadmap most credible for scale; RGTI is pure-play publicly traded.
- **Photonic quantum** (QUBT): Uses photons as qubits. Room-temperature operation advantage. Very early.

- **Key Players**: IONQ (trapped-ion, DoD contracts), QBTS/D-Wave (annealing, commercial customers), RGTI (superconducting, DARPA), QUBT (photonic, early stage)
- **ETF Access**: CQTM, WQTM (pure-play quantum ETFs — diversified entry)
