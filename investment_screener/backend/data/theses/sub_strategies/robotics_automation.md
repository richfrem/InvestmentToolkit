## 2. The Core Thesis (Why this? Why now?)

**The cost collapse**: Industrial robot arms cost $50,000-$200,000 in 2020. New entrants (Figure AI, 1X, Agility, Apptronik) are targeting $10,000-$30,000 general-purpose humanoid robots by 2026-2027. At these price points, the replacement economics for repetitive manual labor become compelling across automotive, logistics, e-commerce, and elder care.

**VLA models as the unlock**: The same transformer architecture that powers ChatGPT can now drive robot bodies. Google DeepMind's RT-2, Figure AI's Helix, and Tesla's Optimus all use VLA models trained on massive video-action datasets. This means robot "intelligence" can be updated via software — the same compounding dynamic that made software companies valuable.

**The humanoid form factor wins**: General-purpose environments (warehouses, hospitals, homes) were designed for human-shaped workers. A humanoid can operate existing equipment, vehicles, and tools without re-engineering the environment. Boston Dynamics, Figure, 1X, and Tesla Optimus are all converging on this form factor for the same reason.

**Why ETFs, not individual stocks**:
- The winning humanoid robot company cannot be identified today. Figure AI, 1X, Agility, Apptronik, and Tesla Optimus are all viable. Several will fail.
- Most pure-play robotics companies are private (Figure AI raised at $2.6B; 1X raised at $100M+). Public market exposure is limited to diversified ETFs.
- At 5-6% total portfolio allocation, splitting across 6-8 individual positions creates unmanageable noise. ETFs auto-rebalance to sector winners.
- This is identical to the playbook used for Applied AI software (owning KOID/HUMN rather than picking individual robotics picks).

**Chosen vehicles**:
- **HUMN** (Roundhill Humanoid Robotics ETF): Pure-play humanoid robot exposure — holds Tesla, Boston Dynamics parent, NVIDIA (robot training), and key humanoid component makers.
- **KOID** (KraneShares Humanoid & AI Robotics ETF): Complementary basket — broader industrial automation + humanoid crossover. Provides diversification across supply chain and software layers.
