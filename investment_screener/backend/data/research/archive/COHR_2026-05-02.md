## Investment Thesis

Coherent Corp's competitive edge is unique in the optical components industry: they are the only company that manufactures compound semiconductor wafers (InP, GaAs, SiC), designs the photonic chips from those wafers, packages them into transceiver modules, and ships finished products — all in-house. This vertical integration delivers a 6-12 month technology cycle advantage over competitors who must source wafers externally.

The 800G to 1.6T optical transceiver upgrade cycle is a multi-year secular growth driver. Every AI training cluster and inference data center requires optical interconnects at scale — and as GPU clusters grow to hundreds of thousands of chips, the bandwidth requirements scale faster than linear. Meta, Google, Microsoft, and Amazon all need more 800G/1.6T transceivers than the market can supply.

The margin trajectory is the entire investment debate. Current 4.66% GAAP margins reflect ~$400-500M/year in merger-related charges from the March 2022 II-VI + Coherent Inc. combination: purchase price amortization, integration consulting fees, facility consolidation costs, and elevated SBC from RSU grants. Pre-merger, II-VI ran 8-12% GAAP margins on a smaller revenue base. As integration completes, the combined $14B+ revenue entity should achieve 18%+ GAAP at scale.

At $329.50, the market has priced in roughly the base case executing. You're not getting the bull case free — forward P/E of 43x is already a premium multiple assuming the margin expansion materializes. HOLD: great company, right price, no margin of safety.

## Key Risks

1. **Integration execution risk**: The II-VI/Coherent merger is one of the largest in optical components history. Integration of manufacturing processes, supply chains, and product roadmaps is complex — execution risk remains elevated 3 years post-close if synergies haven't fully materialized.
2. **Chinese transceiver competition**: InnoLight, Eoptolink, and HGG are all shipping competitive 400G products and closing the gap on 800G. If Chinese vendors achieve 800G parity at 70-80% of COHR pricing, gross margins compress materially. 1.6T is the moat, but 800G is 70%+ of current revenue.
3. **AI capex cycle moderation**: The current extraordinary AI infrastructure build is partly driven by hyperscaler competitive dynamics (each company must match others' buildout). If the capex cycle moderates post-2026-2027, optical transceiver demand growth slows materially.
4. **Convertible dilution**: NI/EPS-derived shares (265M) > outstanding shares (187.5M) suggests potential convertible note dilution not captured in current outstanding count. If convertibles convert, per-share EPS would be ~30% lower than modeled.
5. **Technology obsolescence**: Co-packaged optics (CPO) — where optical transceivers are integrated directly into switch chips — could reduce the module-based transceiver market. Intel, Broadcom, and Marvell are all investing in CPO. COHR's module business faces long-term disruption risk from this architecture shift.
