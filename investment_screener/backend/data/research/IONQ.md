## Investment Thesis

IonQ is the most commercially advanced pure-play quantum computing company. Unlike RGTI (superconducting, competing directly with IBM/Google) and QBTS (annealing, limited TAM), IonQ uses trapped-ion qubits — a technology that achieves higher gate fidelity at comparable qubit counts. This accuracy advantage has enabled real cloud partnerships on AWS Braket, Azure Quantum, and Google Cloud Marketplace.

Revenue growth from $11M to $130M over four years demonstrates genuine commercial traction, not just hype. Government contracts (DoE, Air Force) and enterprise trials provide diversified demand. The bull case is that trapped-ion becomes the dominant QC architecture as the industry scales toward fault-tolerant quantum computing, with IonQ positioned as the "NVIDIA of quantum" — the infrastructure layer that captures most of the value.

The bear case is that IBM and Google's superconducting roadmaps catch up on accuracy, IonQ's pricing power erodes, and gross margins (already declining 73%→40%) continue compressing to near zero. The company currently burns ~$300M in FCF annually and will require continued dilutive capital raises.

The key tension: this is a 10-20 year thesis, but the DCF model runs 5 years. The $17.6B market cap prices in a $100B+ outcome. The model says $8.54. Both can be "right" depending on your time horizon.

## Key Risks
1. **Gross margin compression**: 73%→40% trend suggests hardware costs aren't scaling favorably. If margins reach near-zero, the bull case collapses.
2. **IBM/Google competition**: Both have orders-of-magnitude more R&D budget and will continue improving superconducting accuracy — potentially closing the trapped-ion advantage.
3. **Quantum winter**: If near-term quantum advantage is not demonstrated, institutional interest could collapse and the stock could return to $5-10 (30x revenue for a failing speculative tech).
4. **Dilution**: Burning $300M FCF annually with no profitability path forces continued share issuance, creating headwinds even in bull scenarios.
5. **Technology fork risk**: The industry could standardize on photonic, topological, or neutral-atom approaches — none of which are trapped-ion.
