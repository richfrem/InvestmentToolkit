## Investment Thesis (REIT Methodology Note)

Kilroy Realty Corp is an office REIT that owns premium tech campus properties across San Francisco Bay Area, Los Angeles, and Seattle. Analyzing KRC with standard GAAP EPS-based DCF produces a misleading result: the calculated fair value of $15.74 (SELL, -53%) significantly understates the company's cash-generating ability due to a structural feature of REIT accounting.

Office REITs carry large real estate portfolios on their balance sheets. The $10B+ in gross assets generates $300M+ per year in depreciation charges that flow through the income statement as an expense — even though real estate typically appreciates in value over time. This D&A expense suppresses GAAP net income dramatically. The correct cash generation metric for REITs is **FFO (Funds From Operations)** = net income + D&A + impairments - gains on property sales. KRC's EBITDA margin is 70.5% — far more representative of cash earnings than the GAAP net margin.

Adding back ~$2/share in annual D&A to the analyst's $0.56 GAAP EPS Y1 gives approximately $2.56 FFO/share. At the historical office REIT P/FFO range of 10-18x, that implies $25-46/share — approximately consistent with the analyst HOLD consensus at $35.50 and the current price of $33.64.

The GAAP DCF SELL signal is therefore a methodology limitation for this REIT, not a fundamental business deterioration. The Piotroski 7/9 score (ROA positive and improving, leverage decreasing, current ratio improving) confirms the financial health is strong.

However, the secular headwinds are real. West Coast tech office demand has been impaired by WFH adoption. KRC's tenants (Google, Meta, Salesforce, and Silicon Valley tech companies broadly) have reduced office footprints post-COVID. The AI office recovery thesis is emerging but unproven. If WFH proves structural, office REITs face long-term revenue pressure regardless of balance sheet quality.

## Key Risks

1. **Structural WFH adoption** — If the 2020-2024 WFH shift is permanent (not cyclical), office REIT NOI could decline by 20-30% over a decade as leases expire at lower rates. West Coast tech companies are most exposed.
2. **Higher-for-longer interest rates** — REIT valuations are heavily rate-sensitive. Persistent high rates increase refinancing costs and reduce the spread between NOI yield and borrowing cost.
3. **Lease concentration** — KRC's top-10 tenants likely represent 40-50% of revenue. Loss of any major tenant (Salesforce layoffs reduced SF office demand significantly) would materially impair revenue.
