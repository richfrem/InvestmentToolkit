## Investment Thesis

Oracle's transformation is a prime example of a legacy tech giant successfully pivoting to the cloud. Historically viewed as a mature, slow-growth database provider, Oracle is now a legitimate hyper-scaler through its Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) segment. The market is slowly waking up to the fact that OCI is capturing significant AI training and inference workloads by offering high performance at lower costs compared to AWS and Azure.

This transition is evident in the financials. TTM revenue growth has accelerated to 21.7%, driving the Rule of 40 score to a very healthy 63.36. While free cash flow took a hit recently (likely due to massive capital expenditures required to build out AI data centers), the underlying operating margins remain robust at over 31%. 

At a trailing P/E of ~31x, the market is pricing Oracle somewhere between a legacy value stock and a high-growth cloud provider. We believe this multiple has room to expand as OCI continues to scale and becomes the dominant driver of the business. Our base case DCF model—which assumes a conservative 18% revenue CAGR (below the aggressive analyst consensus of 24%+) and models margin expansion to 26%—yields a fair value of $238.51. This presents a compelling +38.8% margin of safety, making Oracle an attractive BUY for exposure to the AI infrastructure layer with downside protection from its sticky legacy database business.

## Key Risks
1. Cloud infrastructure growth stalls against larger, better-capitalized incumbents (AWS, Azure, GCP).
2. Legacy database migration to open-source alternatives (like PostgreSQL) accelerates.
3. Heavy capex requirements for AI infrastructure pressure near-term free cash flow.
4. Macroeconomic slowdown impacts enterprise IT budgets and delays massive cloud migrations.
