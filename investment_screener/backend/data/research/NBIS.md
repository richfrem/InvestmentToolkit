## Investment Thesis

Nebius Group is one of the most unusual investment cases in global markets: a company that effectively shed its entire operating history when the former Yandex N.V. sold its Russian internet operations in 2024, reconstituting as a pure-play AI infrastructure builder. What remains is a Dutch-listed entity with institutional credibility, a direct NVIDIA strategic alliance, and a rapidly scaling GPU cloud business targeting the global AI infrastructure market.

The core thesis is straightforward: AI infrastructure demand is growing faster than supply, and Nebius is one of the few companies outside the Big Three hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, GCP) positioned to capture meaningful share. With a $3.3B analyst revenue consensus for 2026 — representing 28x its current TTM run-rate — GPU cluster capacity appears to be coming online aggressively. The 2027 consensus of $10.1B (+206% year-over-year) implies this is not a one-quarter phenomenon but a sustained ramp.

The NVIDIA alliance is the critical structural differentiator. Early, preferential access to NVIDIA's latest GPU generations (H100/H200/Blackwell and beyond) provides both a supply advantage and a co-development pathway into NVIDIA's Physical AI roadmap (Omniverse, robotics simulation, autonomous systems). No other independent cloud provider has this level of NVIDIA integration, creating a moat that AWS, Azure, and GCP — with their competing accelerator programs — cannot easily replicate for AI-native workloads.

The counter-thesis is equally compelling: the company has no meaningful operating history as Nebius, is deeply pre-profitable (analyst EPS consensus of -$3.95 in 2026 and -$4.14 in 2027), and is competing against trillion-dollar hyperscalers with vastly deeper pockets. GPU cloud without hyperscaler scale is a capital-intensive business with thin unit economics until utilization rates reach maturity — and Nebius has not yet demonstrated this. The geopolitical overhang (Netherlands-incorporated but Russian asset origin, ongoing sanctions landscape) adds an additional layer of uncertainty that most Western institutional investors will need to be comfortable with.

**Summary verdict**: For investors who believe AI infrastructure will remain supply-constrained for 3-5 years and that NVIDIA's ecosystem advantage extends to its cloud partners, NBIS offers compelling upside at current prices. The position is binary in nature — treat it as a venture-like allocation, not a core holding.

## Key Risks

1. **Hyperscaler dominance**: AWS, Azure, and GCP have vastly deeper capital resources, global infrastructure, and customer relationships. Enterprise AI buyers may consolidate workloads with hyperscalers even if GPU performance is slightly inferior, purely for operational simplicity and integrated billing.

2. **Capital dependency & dilution spiral**: Nebius is burning significant cash to build GPU clusters and is pre-profitable through at least 2027. If equity markets tighten or sentiment shifts, capital raises at dilutive prices could erode per-share value even if the underlying business executes.

3. **GPU supply normalization**: The NVIDIA GPU supply constraint that created Nebius's window of opportunity could ease faster than expected as Samsung, SK Hynix, and TSMC capacity expands — removing the structural advantage of early GPU access.

4. **Geopolitical / regulatory overhang**: Netherlands-incorporated but with Russian asset origin and key personnel. Ongoing geopolitical tensions create incremental regulatory, reputational, and sanctions risk that could deter certain institutional investors or partnerships.

5. **Revenue concentration in ramp period**: The $3.3B 2026 revenue consensus implies massive concentration of capacity coming online — if key customers delay deployments or GPU utilization underperforms expectations, realized revenue could fall dramatically short of analyst models.
