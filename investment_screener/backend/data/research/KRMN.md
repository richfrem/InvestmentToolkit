## Investment Thesis

Karman Holdings is the pickaxe supplier in the hypersonics gold rush. While the Golden Dome narrative rightly focuses on guidance systems, AI targeting, and satellite constellations, somebody has to manufacture the aerodynamic shells, propulsion systems, and interstage structures that physically hold these weapons together at Mach 10+. That's Karman's business. The company serves three end markets: hypersonics and strategic missile defense, tactical missiles and integrated defense systems, and space and launch. All three are under-supplied and facing decades of Pentagon investment.

The financial trajectory is one of the cleanest improvement stories in small-cap defense:
- Gross margins have expanded every single year: 35.8% → 37.6% → 38.3% → 40.3%
- Revenue is compounding at ~28% annual (3-yr CAGR) with analyst consensus projecting a 55% jump in 2026 to $730.9M
- EPS is set to quadruple from $0.13 to $0.57 in 2026 — not from revenue magic, but from the company's PE-era debt load being paid down from IPO proceeds, causing interest expense to drop dramatically and net margins to normalize toward the ~15% operating margin

The complexity in this stock is the valuation framework. A standard 5-year Industrials DCF at 10% discount rate yields ~$18 fair value using sector-appropriate P/E multiples (16-35x). But 10 analysts targeting $105 are applying growth-company logic: a business growing EPS at 60%+ per year commands a premium multiple (40-80x forward earnings). Both frameworks capture something true. The DCF is conservative. The analyst targets require perfect execution. The stock at $57.50 (down 38% from highs) sits squarely in the middle.

## The PE Leverage Unlock (Key to Understanding 2026E)

This is the single most important thing to understand about KRMN's earnings trajectory:

| Year | Revenue | Operating Margin | Operating Income | Interest Expense (est.) | Net Margin |
|------|---------|----------------|-----------------|------------------------|------------|
| 2022 | $226M | 9.0% | $20.4M | ~$53M (PE debt) | -6.2% |
| 2023 | $281M | 17.3% | $48.6M | ~$43M | +1.6% |
| 2024 | $345M | 18.4% | $63.5M | ~$51M | +3.7% |
| 2025 TTM | $472M | 15.5% | $73.1M | ~$55M | +3.7% |
| 2026E | $731M | ~15.5% | $113.3M | ~$10M (post-IPO) | ~10.3% |

The company's operating margin generates real profit. The net margin "problem" has been $50-55M of annual interest expense from PE-era leverage. IPO proceeds paid this down. In 2026, the same operating margin (~15.5%) on $731M revenue generates $113M operating income — and with interest expense at ~$10M, tax at ~28M, net income jumps to ~$75M → 10.3% net margin → $0.57/share EPS.

This is NOT a margin improvement story from operations. It is a capital structure normalization. The business was always earning solid returns; the PE structure was eating all the profit. Now it won't.

## Key Risks

1. **Operating margin decline despite gross margin expansion**: TTM operating margin fell from 18.4% to 15.5% even as gross margins improved — SG&A is scaling faster than expected. If this trend continues rather than reverses at scale, the margin normalization thesis is weaker.

2. **FCF divergence**: TTM FCF of -$42.5M despite $17.4M net income. The gap likely reflects working capital build for the $730M 2026E ramp or capex. Needs to turn positive for valuation to hold.

3. **PE sponsor exit overhang**: TCFIII Spaceco SPV LP remains a majority owner. Staged exits create sustained selling pressure that can drag the stock regardless of fundamental performance.

4. **Single-customer concentration**: Defense structural components are often awarded to specific contractors per program. Loss of one major program (e.g., if GBSD schedule slips by 3 years) could remove 20-30% of the revenue pipeline with no near-term replacement.

5. **Re-rating risk from CAGR deceleration**: The 62.4x forward P/E embeds 60%+ EPS growth. The moment this slows meaningfully (say to 25%), the multiple could compress sharply — stock could fall 30-40% even with earnings growth.
