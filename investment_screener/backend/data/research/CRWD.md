## Investment Thesis

CrowdStrike built something rare: a security platform with genuine network effects. Every Falcon sensor contributes threat telemetry that improves detection for all 29,000+ customers. The AI models trained on this dataset are a competitive moat that strengthens with scale — competitors face an ever-widening data gap. Add in endpoint switching costs (removing a security sensor from every device in an enterprise is a 12-18 month project CISOs will avoid unless forced) and you have a durable business.

The problem is the price. At $455 (73x forward P/E on negative GAAP earnings), CrowdStrike requires not just bull case execution but bull case execution *and* premium multiple retention. The base case — 20% revenue CAGR improving to 10% GAAP margins at a 35x exit multiple — produces a fair value of $102 after discounting 5 years at 10%. To justify $455 today, you essentially need the bull case (28% CAGR, 18% margins, 50x P/E) to be near-certainty. We weight that at 25%.

The Microsoft threat is real but often overstated. Microsoft Defender bundling captures mid-market accounts, but enterprise security teams have learned the hard way that free/bundled security is not sufficient for sophisticated threats. CrowdStrike's premium customers (F500, government, financial services) are highly unlikely to switch to Defender. But it does cap CrowdStrike's TAM expansion in mid-market — a meaningful growth headwind.

Watch for re-entry below $250, where even the base case starts to provide reasonable return potential.

## Key Risks

1. **Microsoft Defender displacement**: MSFT has 400M+ M365 seats with Defender included free. Mid-market companies with limited security budgets increasingly accept "good enough" free EDR. This caps CrowdStrike's TAM expansion — not an existential threat to existing enterprise customers, but a meaningful growth headwind.
2. **Multiple compression**: The biggest near-term risk is pure multiple contraction. If rate cycles tighten or risk sentiment shifts, 73x → 25x P/E represents a 65% price decline without any deterioration in fundamentals.
3. **GAAP margin normalization timeline**: SBC declining from ~20% of revenue requires headcount growth to slow meaningfully. If CrowdStrike continues hiring aggressively to chase the AI security opportunity, GAAP profitability may take longer than our 5-year model assumes.
4. **AI-native competition**: CrowdStrike's AI advantage is based on data scale (1M+ sensors). If adversaries leverage LLMs to generate novel malware at scale (AI-generated attack surface expansion), the threat detection arms race accelerates — requiring more R&D spend, not less.
5. **SentinelOne + Palo Alto competition**: Both competitors have viable endpoint platforms and are pricing aggressively to capture share during the platform consolidation phase.
