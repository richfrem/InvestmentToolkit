## Investment Thesis

### Moat #1: Oracle Database Switching Costs
400,000+ Oracle databases run worldwide in banks, hospitals, governments, and Fortune 500 companies. These databases hold decades of financial records, clinical data, and operational history. Migrating from Oracle DB is not a technical project — it is an existential risk management decision. The multi-year timeline, $10M-$100M cost, and operational risk means the vast majority of Oracle DB customers will never leave. This moat has been tested for 40+ years and remains fully intact.

### Moat #2: Oracle Fusion ERP/HCM Switching Costs
Oracle Fusion Cloud ERP, HCM, and SCM are deeply embedded in enterprise workflows. Once a company has re-engineered its HR, finance, and supply chain processes on Oracle Fusion, the switching cost is comparable to ripping out an enterprise's central nervous system. Post-implementation retention rates exceed 95%. This moat is younger than the database moat but compounding as Fusion cloud adoption accelerates.

### Margin Expansion: Four Consecutive Years of Improvement
- FY2022: 15.8% → FY2023: 17.0% → FY2024: 19.8% → FY2025: 21.7%
The direction is unambiguous. As on-premise perpetual license revenue (lower margin, declining) is replaced by cloud SaaS subscription revenue (higher margin, growing), the blended margin structural trajectory is upward. At 21.7% TTM, Oracle is still 8pp below best-in-class SaaS (30%+) — the runway for continued expansion is substantial.

### OCI: The AI Cloud Wildcard
Oracle Cloud Infrastructure has disclosed $97B+ in remaining performance obligations (RPO). The analyst revenue estimate jump from +17.1% (Y1) to +31.6% (Y2) likely reflects this RPO beginning to convert to recognized revenue. OCI's differentiation: uniquely suited for AI workloads that need proximity to Oracle databases, and priced meaningfully below AWS/Azure for many database-adjacent applications. This is not a guaranteed catalyst but is a real and growing revenue driver.

## Key Risks

1. **OCI capex cycle duration** — FY2025 FCF was -$394M despite $12.44B NI, signaling a large datacenter investment cycle. If this persists at elevated levels, shareholder returns (buybacks, dividends) are constrained and the financial picture looks weaker than income statement suggests.
2. **Competitive cloud market** — AWS, Azure, and GCP have $200B+ annual cloud revenue collectively and are investing aggressively. Oracle winning a large share of AI workloads is possible but not guaranteed.
3. **Gross margin dilution** — OCI runs at lower gross margins than Oracle's pure software business. Continued OCI growth at scale will continue the structural gross margin decline (79% → 70.5% over 4 years).
4. **Debt load** — Oracle has taken on significant debt to fund acquisitions (Cerner, etc.) and OCI buildout. Rising rates increase interest expense, partially offsetting operating leverage.
