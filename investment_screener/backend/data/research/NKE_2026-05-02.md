## Investment Thesis

Nike is facing one of the most challenging periods in its corporate history. Once the undisputed king of global athletic apparel and footwear, the company is experiencing a profound growth stall. TTM revenue is completely flat (0.1% growth), and the initial analyst consensus for the next 12 months implies negative growth. 

More concerning is the margin compression. Net margins have degraded from historical highs of ~13% down to just 7%. This compression is a direct result of increased promotional activity required to clear stale inventory and a highly competitive retail environment. Agile, specialized competitors like On Holding and Hoka have successfully captured market share in the premium running category, traditionally a Nike stronghold. The resulting Rule of 40 score of 9.84 highlights this severe fundamental deterioration.

While the stock has already suffered a massive drawdown (down >60% over 5 years) to $44.40, it is still trading at an elevated ~29x trailing P/E multiple. The market is pricing in a swift and robust recovery. However, our DCF model, which assumes a 1% revenue CAGR and a modest margin recovery to 8% exiting at a 20x multiple, yields an intrinsic value of $40.01. Nike's iconic brand provides a floor, but it does not offer a margin of safety at current levels. The stock is a SELL.

1. Continued market share loss to On, Hoka, and other specialized brands in premium categories.
2. Severe recessionary pullback in discretionary consumer spending globally.
3. Inability to reignite the product innovation cycle, leading to brand fatigue.
4. Margin recovery is slower than expected due to Direct-To-Consumer (DTC) transition hiccups and reliance on wholesale partners.
