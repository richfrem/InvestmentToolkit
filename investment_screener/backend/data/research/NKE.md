## Investment Thesis

Nike is the most iconic sports brand in history and it is currently in the worst operational position of its modern era. Understanding why this happened is essential to assessing whether the turnaround is credible.

Under CEO John Donahoe (2020-2024), Nike executed an aggressive Direct-to-Consumer pivot — aggressively cutting wholesale relationships with Foot Locker, DSW, Academy Sports, and hundreds of independent running stores in favor of its own digital channels. The strategic logic was sound in theory (better margins, richer customer data, brand control), but the execution had catastrophic side effects: Nike lost its presence on the physical retail floors where consumers discover new products. Simultaneously, the innovation pipeline stalled — Nike continued milking franchises (Air Force 1, Dunks, classic Jordan styles) rather than developing genuinely new performance products. Competitors filled the vacuum ruthlessly. On Running went from ~$300M to $2.5B+ in revenue. Hoka became the dominant premium running brand. New Balance staged a cultural comeback.

New CEO Elliott Hill (Nike veteran, returned October 2024) has reversed the DTC-over-wholesale obsession and is rebuilding wholesale relationships. But product development cycles are 18-24 months. FY2026 results will still largely reflect Donahoe-era products. FY2027-2028 is when Hill's product bets either pay off or don't. Analyst consensus reflects this: FY2026 revenue flat (-0.1%), FY2026 EPS $1.50 — *below* the TTM $2.17. The pain continues before any recovery.

The valuation math produces a HOLD at current prices. The base case DCF ($43.67) is essentially equal to the current stock price ($44.40) — the market appears to be pricing a modest turnaround recovery accurately. This means HOLD is the correct call: there's no meaningful margin of safety for a BUY, but the brand moat and turnaround optionality prevent a SELL. We differ from the prior Gemini SELL thesis ($40.01) by applying a higher base scenario weight and acknowledging that $44.40 is approximately fair for a turnaround scenario.

The asymmetry is wide: bear $12 (brand erosion accelerates), bull $111 (Hill executes a full innovation reset). This wide range reflects genuine uncertainty about a turnaround still in its first year.

## Key Risks

1. **Turnaround timeline uncertainty** — Hill's product pipeline won't show results until FY2027-2028. Two more years of fundamental weakness are almost certain before recovery signals.

2. **Structural competitive share loss** — On Running, Hoka, and New Balance captured consumers with genuinely better products. These consumers may not return even if Nike improves. Athletic footwear brand preferences are sticky.

3. **China deterioration** — Nike China revenue declining amid domestic brand competition (Anta, Li-Ning) and anti-Western sentiment. Geopolitical escalation could further impair the most important international growth market.

4. **Margin compression acceleration** — Analyst FY2026 EPS $1.50 implies further compression to 4.8% from 6.95% TTM. If FY2026 disappoints against even this reduced expectation, confidence in recovery collapses.

5. **Depressed earnings PE trap** — At 29.2x trailing PE on severely depressed EPS, Nike looks expensive. If the market recalibrates to current EPS rather than recovery EPS, multiple compression could push shares below $30.
