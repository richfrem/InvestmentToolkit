## Investment Thesis
Palantir has completed a genuine transformation from a decade of government-funded losses into a highly profitable software platform. Net margin has expanded every year for four consecutive years (-19.6% → 9.4% → 16.1% → 36.3% → 43.7% TTM), and gross margin sits above 82% — both consistent with a company that has found real operating leverage, not a one-time accounting artifact. The moat is structural: Gotham and Foundry create deep ontology-based lock-in across government and enterprise customers, with high switching costs once an organization's data model is built on the platform.

The problem is entirely valuation, not fundamentals. At 147-154x trailing earnings, the market has already priced in years of continued hypergrowth and margin expansion. FY2026 analyst consensus calls for $7.72B revenue (+72.5% YoY) and FY2027 for $11.20B (+45.1% YoY) — genuinely strong, but even fully crediting that trajectory, a disciplined 5-year DCF with a sector-appropriate exit multiple (capped at 65x even in the bull case, vs. today's 147-154x) lands at $147.06, only modestly above the current price.

This is a name where the story and the price have converged. It's a HOLD for existing holders, not an initiation at today's price for new capital — the risk/reward improves meaningfully on any pullback that restores a margin of safety.

## Investment Thesis

Palantir is the most compelling AI software story in public markets and also one of the most dangerous stocks to own at current valuations. Both statements are simultaneously true. The company has built two genuinely differentiated products: Gotham (government intelligence analysis, used by CIA, NSA, US military, allied intelligence services) and the Artificial Intelligence Platform (AIP), a commercial product that integrates enterprise data with large language models in a governed, explainable framework. The AIP bootcamp model — 5-day intensive deployments that demonstrate ROI before contracts are signed — has been extraordinarily effective at converting skeptical enterprise buyers. Commercial customer count has been growing at 50%+ rates quarterly.

The financial metrics confirm this is not hype. The progression from -19.6% net margin (FY2022) to 9.4% (FY2023) to 16.1% (FY2024) to 36.3% (TTM) represents a genuine profitability inflection, not accounting manipulation. FCF has grown from $184M to $697M to $1.14B to $2.10B over four years — every year higher. Revenue CAGR over 4 years is approximately 32%. This is an exceptional business.

The problem is entirely valuation. At $144/share and 228x trailing PE, the market has fully priced this quality into the stock and is reaching forward to price the next 5 years of base-case growth as well. Our DCF base case — 34% CAGR for 5 years (analyst-grounded via deceleration path), 35% margins, 45x exit PE — yields $88.37/share in present value. The Year 5 undiscounted base-case price of $142.31 is nearly identical to today's price of $144.07. This is the "priced for perfection" signal: buying today earns zero time-value compensation even if PLTR executes perfectly on the base case.

Asymmetry is unfavorable at current price. Bear case (-87% to $18.57) is 3× the magnitude of the bull case upside (+42% to $204.81). The weighted average of these outcomes is $96.54 — 33% below market price.

## Key Risks

1. **Government contract concentration and opacity** (primary): Government revenue (~55% historically) consists of classified contracts with opaque renewal schedules. The NHS cancellation (2022) demonstrated contracts can evaporate. US defense budget uncertainty under shifting administrations is a permanent risk.

2. **AIP competitive displacement**: AWS Bedrock, Azure AI Studio, and Google Vertex AI are all building enterprise AI platforms with the advantage of existing cloud infrastructure lock-in. If hyperscalers release "good enough" AI workflow tools embedded in their existing enterprise agreements, AIP's differentiation is challenged.

3. **Multiple compression risk**: Trading at 228x trailing and 77x forward PE, PLTR has essentially no valuation cushion. Any earnings miss or guidance reduction could compress the multiple dramatically — independent of business quality.

4. **SBC dilution history**: PLTR has historically issued massive stock-based compensation. The Piotroski score flags leverage increasing. If SBC re-accelerates (e.g., to retain talent as competitors raise packages), GAAP margins compress materially.

5. **Classified program dependency**: A significant portion of PLTR's government revenue comes from classified programs that cannot be discussed publicly. This creates information asymmetry — investors cannot independently assess the durability of this revenue.

---

## What to Watch

- **Commercial customer count growth** (quarterly): The most important leading indicator — if commercial AIP customers grow at 40%+ quarterly, the bull case strengthens
- **Government contract wins/renewals**: Watch for press releases on major DoD, intelligence community contracts; these can shift the revenue profile materially
- **AIP revenue mix**: As AIP grows, watch whether it displaces Gotham revenue or adds incrementally (determines margin trajectory)
- **SBC as % of revenue**: If SBC declines as % of revenue, margin improvement is sustainable; if it re-inflates, bear case margin assumptions become more likely
- **Hyperscaler AI platform adoption**: Monitor AWS/Azure/Google enterprise AI product announcements — any sign of commoditizing PLTR's core AIP functionality is a major bear signal
