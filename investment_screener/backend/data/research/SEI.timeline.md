## Investment Thesis

Solaris Energy Infrastructure (formerly Solaris Oilfield Infrastructure) is executing one of the most impressive business pivots in the industrial sector. Originally focused on modular equipment for oilfield material management (sand, water), the company identified that its core competency — deploying modular, scalable, rapidly-deployable equipment-based solutions — was perfectly applicable to data center power infrastructure.

The pivot is working: revenue grew from $313M (FY2024) to $622M (TTM), a 99% increase. Analysts project $825M Y1 (+32.7%) and $1.22B Y2 (+47.4%). The Rule of 40 score of 85 (55% revenue growth + 30% EBITDA margin) is exceptional for an industrial company and rivals SaaS businesses.

The data center power thesis is straightforward: AI/ML workloads require massive, reliable power delivery at scale. Traditional utility grid connections take 3-7 years to permit and build. Modular, deployable power solutions from companies like SEI compress that timeline to months. Hyperscalers (Amazon AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud) and frontier AI labs are willing to pay premium prices for speed of deployment.

The negative FCF (-$438M TTM) is the primary concern. SEI is deploying capital aggressively to build out the modular equipment fleet to serve data center contracts. This is analogous to a SaaS company investing in engineering headcount to capture contracts — the payoff comes when contracted revenue exceeds capex. However, if data center demand disappoints or contracts are cancelled, SEI faces stranded capital risk.

Analyst Strong Buy consensus at $83.57 (12 analysts) vs current $72.33 provides external validation of the thesis. Our DCF base case at $57.08 PV (28% CAGR, 10% margin, 25x PE) weighted with a strong bull case ($281.76) produces a weighted FV of $86.94 — a BUY.

## Key Risks

1. **Capex cycle risk** — $438M negative FCF in TTM. If data center contracts slow or are delayed, SEI faces significant stranded capital in modular equipment. Asset-heavy business model in a potentially cyclical market.
2. **Share count ambiguity** — Multiple share count figures (43.7M diluted, 58.1M outstanding, 93.1M mktcap-implied) suggest a complex capital structure. Investors should verify diluted share count directly before sizing positions.
3. **Competition from utilities and large industrials** — Caterpillar, Cummins, and large power generation companies may enter the modular data center power segment with larger scale and existing customer relationships.
