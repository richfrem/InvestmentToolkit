## Investment Thesis

### The Backlog Moat
Symbotic's $23B+ backlog — approximately 10+ years of revenue at current $2.25B pace — is the foundational investment thesis. This is not speculative pipeline; it is contracted, committed spend from Walmart and other retail customers for warehouse automation system deployments. Once deployed, the switching costs are enormous: replacing an installed Symbotic system requires multi-year supply chain disruption, $50M+ in redeployment costs, and complete workforce retraining. This creates structural revenue visibility and a durable customer retention moat.

### Operating Leverage: The Make-or-Break Variable
Symbotic currently spends ~22% of revenue on SG&A and ~23% on R&D as a percentage of revenue — fixed-cost-heavy spending that hasn't scaled down with revenue growth. At 3x revenue scale (Year 5 base: $6.86B vs TTM $2.25B), simple arithmetic suggests these percentages must compress. The base case assumes:
- Gross margins improve from 18.8% to ~23% as recurring software/services revenue grows
- SG&A scales from ~22% to ~15% of revenue
- R&D investment plateaus in absolute terms as platform matures
- Net margin reaches 7% — achievable with these three levers

The bear case challenges each assumption: gross margins stagnate (hardware-heavy mix persists), SG&A doesn't scale (need to keep hiring for new deployments), and the company remains near-breakeven.

### Walmart: Captive Customer and Strategic Partner
Walmart's deep integration into Symbotic (via Class V ownership stake + system-wide deployment commitment) is both a moat and a concentration risk. As the anchor deployment, Walmart de-risks revenue growth while providing a reference customer that accelerates enterprise sales. The negative interpretation: if Walmart's capex plans change, 40-50%+ of Symbotic's near-term revenue visibility could evaporate.

## Key Risks

1. **Operating leverage fails** — Most critical risk. The company has never been profitable. If gross margins don't improve beyond 19% and SG&A doesn't compress at scale, the bear case ($9.08 PV = -85%) is the outcome.
2. **Walmart customer concentration** — If Walmart's capex plans slow or shift, the backlog visibility deteriorates dramatically. Limited diversification at this stage is a structural vulnerability.
3. **Equity dilution** — The company regularly issues shares to fund operations and compensate employees. At a -0.75% net margin, free cash flow is constrained, requiring ongoing dilution.
4. **Competitive displacement** — Amazon Robotics (internal), Ocado, AutoStore, and Locus Robotics all compete for warehouse automation budgets. A major enterprise loss to a competitor would damage the bull case narrative.
5. **Class V unit exchanges** — As Walmart's Warehouse Technologies entity exchanges Class V units for Class A shares, the Class A share count grows, diluting existing holders.

## What to Watch

- **Quarterly gross margin trajectory** — Critical leading indicator; needs to expand toward 22-24% for base case validity
- **New enterprise customer announcements** — Any win beyond Walmart dramatically validates the bull case
- **Software/recurring revenue as % of total** — Target 20%+ for base case; 30%+ for bull case re-rating
- **SG&A as % of revenue** — Should decline consistently as systems scale; flat or rising = bear signal
- **FCF generation** — Company's $787M FCF in FY2025 was positive; sustained positive FCF is prerequisite for margin confidence
