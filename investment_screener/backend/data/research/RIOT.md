## ⚠️ Methodology Note: GAAP DCF vs BTC NAV

RIOT is a Bitcoin mining company. Analyzing it with standard GAAP EPS-based DCF produces misleading results for the same structural reason as HUT8:

1. **BTC mark-to-market**: GAAP forces unrealized BTC gains/losses through the income statement. RIOT holds ~18,000+ BTC on its balance sheet. A 10% BTC price swing creates $180M+ of GAAP income or loss completely unrelated to mining operations.

2. **Mining revenue is real but modest**: $647M TTM in mining and engineering revenues represents the actual operational cash flow of the business.

3. **Market methodology**: Analysts use BTC NAV = (BTC holdings × BTC price) + (hash rate × estimated BTC mined per year × forward BTC price) / WACC. This is why 19 analysts say Strong Buy at $25.39 despite GAAP losses.

**Our $1.93 DCF is a fundamental floor — the minimum value if BTC is $0 and mining barely breaks even. It is NOT a realistic fair value for a company holding BTC at $80-100K.**

## Investment Thesis

Riot Platforms operates one of the largest Bitcoin mining facilities in North America, with approximately 24 EH/s of deployed hash rate. The business generates $647M in annual revenue from mining operations and an engineering segment (Luxor Technology-style custom ASIC development and deployment services).

The thesis for RIOT is almost entirely a BTC price bet: if BTC goes to $200K+, RIOT's hash rate generates extraordinary revenue, its BTC holdings appreciate dramatically, and the stock re-rates. If BTC stagnates or declines, the stock faces severe fundamental headwinds from fixed infrastructure costs, dilutive equity issuance (ATM), and electricity cost pressures.

The critical structural bear case for RIOT as a long-term holding: management continuously issues equity via ATM offerings to fund operations and expansion. This means BTC bull run gains are partially captured by new shares, not existing holders. The share count grew meaningfully year-over-year. Long-term holders get structurally diluted regardless of BTC performance.

Analyst Y1 revenue of $658M (+1.6%) and Y2 $821M (+24.8%) implies mining revenue grows with BTC prices and hash rate expansion, but analysts are not modeling on GAAP earnings — they're modeling on BTC price levels and mining economics. The Piotroski 3/9 confirms the GAAP financial quality is poor (negative ROA, negative CFO, ROA not improving).

3. **Electricity cost exposure** — Mining margins are primarily a function of BTC price vs electricity cost. A sustained power cost increase or hash rate difficulty surge could make operations uneconomical.
