## Why 12.75%

The model discounts future earnings per common share, so the rate is a cost of equity: 5.15% risk-free + 1.81 beta × 4.20% equity risk premium = 12.75%.

- **Risk-free 5.15%:** US 10-year Treasury par yield, October 7, 2026. [Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value_month=202610)
- **Equity risk premium 4.20%:** Damodaran implied premium for the S&P 500, October 1, 2026, trailing-twelve-month adjusted payout. [Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/home.htm)
- **Beta 1.81:** Damodaran Computer Services unlevered beta 0.96 (January 2026 data), relevered for APLD's $6.38B book debt against $7.20B market value with no tax shield. [Industry betas](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/Betas.html)
- **Rejected:** APLD's own trading beta, which ranges from 2.83 (two years daily) to 5.42 (five years monthly) and spans its shift from crypto hosting to AI data centres. It would give about 17% and a fair value of $27.29.

The result is close to the inherited 12.77% by coincidence; it does not explain how that older figure was produced. The peer beta is hand-derived, the simple debt and equity structure leaves out the noncontrolling interest and preferred stock (treating them as debt-like gives roughly 14%), and the trading-beta regression has no standard error.
